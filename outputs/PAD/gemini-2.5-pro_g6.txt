Peripheral Artery Disease, also called PAD, is a common problem with blood vessels. It happens when the blood vessels that carry blood away from your heart to other parts of your body become narrow or blocked. This usually affects the arteries that send blood to your arms and legs.

You may have heard of a heart attack, which is a blockage of blood flow to the heart. A similar kind of blockage can happen in the arteries of your arms and legs. When this happens, your limbs do not get enough oxygen and nutrients from your blood.

If PAD is not treated, it can become very serious. In some cases, it may lead to the loss of a limb (amputation).

Healthy arteries are open and let blood flow easily. As we get older, a sticky substance called plaque can build up inside our arteries. Plaque is made of fat and cholesterol. This buildup makes the arteries narrow and hard, which makes it difficult for blood to get through.

A common sign of PAD is pain in your legs or arms when you are active, like when you walk. This pain, which can feel like cramping, usually goes away when you stop and rest. This happens because your muscles are not getting enough blood and oxygen when you move.

As PAD gets worse, you might feel pain in your legs or feet even when you are resting, especially at night. You might also notice sores on your feet or legs that do not heal. These are signs that your limbs are not getting the blood they need.

Several things can increase your risk for PAD. Getting older is a risk factor, and men are more likely to have it than women.

Health problems like high blood pressure, diabetes, and high cholesterol also raise your risk. Your daily habits matter too. Smoking, being overweight, and not getting enough exercise can lead to PAD. Having a family history of blood vessel problems also increases your chances of getting it.

Not all leg pain is caused by PAD. But if you are older and have any of the signs of PAD, you should talk to your doctor.

It is very important to see a doctor right away if you have pain when you are resting or have sores on your feet that will not heal. Your doctor may send you to a specialist called a vascular surgeon.

Doctors can find out if you have PAD with simple, painless tests. These tests may include using sound waves (ultrasound) to look at your blood flow and checking the blood pressure in your legs.

A vascular surgeon is a doctor who specializes in treating blood vessel problems. They can help manage your PAD with medicine. If needed, they can also do procedures to improve blood flow, from simple ones with small cuts to more complex surgery.

Treatment for PAD often starts with lifestyle changes. This includes quitting smoking and getting regular exercise. Your doctor may also give you medicine to help.

It is important to treat PAD to improve blood flow. This can prevent serious problems like losing a limb.
