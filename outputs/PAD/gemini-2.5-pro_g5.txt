Peripheral Artery Disease, or PAD, is a problem with the blood vessels that carry blood away from your heart to other parts of your body. It usually affects the arteries that send blood to your legs and arms.

You may have heard of blocked arteries in the heart, which can cause a heart attack. PAD is when you have blocked arteries in other places, like your legs. This means your arms and legs may not get enough blood and oxygen.

Healthy arteries are open, so blood can flow through them easily. Over time, a sticky substance called plaque can build up inside your arteries. Plaque is made of fat and cholesterol. This buildup makes the arteries narrow and hard, which makes it difficult for blood to get through.

A common sign of PAD is pain in your legs or arms when you are active, like when you walk. The pain might feel like cramping. This pain usually goes away when you stop and rest. This happens because your muscles are not getting enough blood and oxygen when you move.

If PAD gets worse, you might feel pain in your legs or feet even when you are resting, especially at night. You might also get sores on your feet that do not heal. These are signs that your limbs are not getting the blood they need.

Certain things can increase your chance of getting PAD. These include getting older, having high blood pressure, diabetes, or high cholesterol. Smoking is also a major risk.

Your risk is also higher if you are overweight, do not get enough exercise, or if others in your family have had blood vessel problems. Men are more likely to get PAD than women.

Not all leg pain is from PAD. But if you are older and have any of the signs of PAD, you should talk to your doctor. Ask your doctor if you should be checked for it.

It is very important to see a doctor right away if you have pain when you are resting or have sores on your feet that will not heal. Your doctor may send you to a specialist called a vascular surgeon.

Doctors can find out if you have PAD with simple, painless tests. They may use a special sound wave test (ultrasound) or check the blood pressure in your legs. These tests show how well blood is flowing.

If you have signs of PAD, your main doctor might send you to a vascular surgeon. This is a doctor who specializes in blood vessel problems. They can give you medicine or perform treatments to help improve your blood flow.

Treatment for PAD often includes making healthy life changes. This means quitting smoking and getting regular exercise. Your doctor might also give you medicine.

If PAD is more serious, treatment is needed to save your leg or arm from being removed (amputation). Getting treatment is very important for your health.
