You have two large blood vessels in your neck called carotid arteries. They carry blood from your heart to your brain. When these arteries are healthy, blood flows easily to give your brain the oxygen and food it needs to work.

As you get older, a sticky substance called plaque can build up inside these arteries. Plaque is made of fat and cholesterol. This buildup makes the arteries narrow and hard.

This condition is called carotid artery disease. It can block blood flow to the brain and may cause a stroke or a "mini-stroke."

You may not have any signs of this disease at first. A stroke could be the first sign that something is wrong. Sometimes, people have warning signs called mini-strokes before a big stroke happens.

The signs of a mini-stroke usually last for a short time, from a few minutes to a few hours. A mini-stroke is a serious medical emergency. You should get help right away.

Signs of a stroke or mini-stroke can happen suddenly. You might feel weakness, numbness, or a tingling feeling on one side of your body. You may not be able to control an arm or a leg.

Other signs include blurry vision or losing sight in one or both eyes. You might also have trouble speaking clearly or find it hard to understand what people are saying.

An easy way to remember the signs of a stroke is the word FAST. F is for face drooping. A is for arm weakness. S is for speech difficulty. T means it is time to call 911.

Several things can increase your risk for this disease. These include getting older, having high blood pressure, or having high cholesterol.

Other risks are smoking, having diabetes, and not getting enough exercise. If others in your family have had this disease, your risk may be higher.

If your doctor thinks you might have carotid artery disease, they may order a special test. This test is an ultrasound of your neck, which uses sound waves to see your arteries and does not hurt. The test shows if your arteries are blocked and how serious the blockage is.

Your doctor may send you to see a special doctor called a vascular surgeon. This doctor is an expert in treating problems with blood vessels. They can help decide the best treatment for you.

Treatment depends on how serious your condition is. Your doctor might suggest changes like eating healthier or exercising more. You may also need to take medicine.

For serious blockages, a procedure may be needed. One option is a surgery to clean the plaque out of the artery. Another option is putting a small tube, called a stent, in the artery to hold it open. Sometimes, surgery is helpful even if you do not have any symptoms.
