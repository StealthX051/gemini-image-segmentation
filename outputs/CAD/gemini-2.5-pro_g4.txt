You have two large blood vessels in your neck called carotid arteries. They carry blood from your heart to your brain. When these blood vessels are healthy, blood flows easily to give your brain the oxygen and food it needs.

As you get older, a sticky substance called plaque can build up inside these blood vessels. Plaque is made of fat and other things in your blood. This buildup makes the blood vessels narrow and hard.

This problem is called carotid artery disease. When the blood vessels get too narrow, it can cause a stroke or a "mini-stroke."

You may not have any signs of this disease at first. A stroke might be the first sign that something is wrong. Sometimes, a person has a "mini-stroke" first, which is a warning sign for a bigger stroke.

The signs of a mini-stroke may only last for a short time. Even if the signs go away, it is a serious emergency. You need to get help right away.

Signs of a stroke or mini-stroke can happen suddenly. You might feel weak or numb on one side of your body. You may not be able to move an arm or a leg.

Your sight might get blurry, or you could have trouble seeing. It might be hard to speak clearly or to understand what people are saying.

A good way to remember the signs of a stroke is the word FAST. F is for Face Drooping, when one side of the face sags. A is for Arm Weakness, when one arm is weak or numb.

S is for Speech Difficulty, when it is hard to talk. T is for Time to call 911. If you see any of these signs, call 911 right away.

Some things make you more likely to get this disease. These include getting older, being overweight, and not getting enough exercise.

Having high blood pressure, high cholesterol, or diabetes also raises your risk. Smoking and having family members with this disease are also risk factors.

If your doctor thinks you might have this disease, they may do a special test. This test is an ultrasound, which uses sound waves to look at your blood vessels. It does not hurt and shows if your blood vessels are narrow.

Your doctor might send you to see a special doctor called a vascular surgeon. This doctor is an expert in treating problems with blood vessels. They can help decide the best treatment for you.

Treatment depends on how serious your condition is. Your doctor may suggest changes like eating healthier or getting more exercise. You might also need to take medicine.

In some cases, a procedure is needed. One type of surgery cleans the plaque out of the blood vessel. Another treatment uses a small tube, called a stent, to hold the blood vessel open. Sometimes, treatment is needed even if you do not have any signs of a problem.
