The carotid arteries are two large blood vessels in your neck. They carry blood from your heart to your brain. A healthy brain needs this blood flow to get oxygen and nutrients.

As you get older, a sticky substance called plaque can build up inside these arteries. Plaque is made of fat and cholesterol. This buildup makes the arteries narrow and stiff, which is sometimes called "hardening of the arteries."

This condition is known as carotid artery disease. It can block blood flow to the brain and cause a stroke.

In the beginning, you may not have any symptoms of carotid artery disease. A stroke could be the first sign that something is wrong.

Sometimes, a person has warning signs called mini-strokes before a major stroke happens. A mini-stroke has symptoms that last for a short time. A mini-stroke is a serious medical emergency that needs immediate care.

Signs of a stroke or mini-stroke can happen suddenly. You might feel weakness, numbness, or a tingling feeling on one side of your body. You may not be able to control an arm or a leg.

Other signs include blurry vision or losing sight in one or both eyes. You might also have trouble speaking clearly or understanding what people are saying.

It is important to know the warning signs of a stroke. An easy way to remember them is the word FAST. F is for Face Drooping, when one side of the face sags. A is for Arm Weakness, when one arm drifts downward.

S is for Speech Difficulty, when speech is slurred or strange. T is for Time to call 911. If you see any of these signs, call for help right away.

Several things can increase your risk for carotid artery disease. These include getting older, having high blood pressure, or having high cholesterol. Other risk factors are smoking, diabetes, and not getting enough exercise. Having a family history of the disease or being overweight can also increase your risk.

If your doctor thinks you might have carotid artery disease, they may order a special test. This test is an ultrasound that uses sound waves to look at the blood flow in your neck arteries. It is a painless test that can show if your arteries are narrowed and how serious the blockage is.

If you have carotid artery disease, you may see a special doctor called a vascular surgeon. These surgeons are experts in treating problems with blood vessels. They can help decide the best treatment for you.

Your treatment will depend on how narrow your arteries are. Your doctor may suggest lifestyle changes like eating a healthy diet and exercising. You might also need to take medicine.

For more serious blockages, a procedure may be needed. One type of surgery involves cleaning the plaque out of the artery. Another option is placing a small tube, called a stent, inside the artery to keep it open. Sometimes, surgery is helpful even for people who do not have any symptoms.
