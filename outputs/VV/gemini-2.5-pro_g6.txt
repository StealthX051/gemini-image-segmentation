Varicose veins are swollen, twisted veins that you can see close to the skin on your legs. They often look blue or purple. High pressure inside the leg veins is a common cause. You are also more likely to get them if other people in your family have them.

Many people in the United States have varicose veins. If you do not treat them, they can get worse. They may cause your legs to ache or feel tired. You might also get skin rashes, redness, or sores called ulcers.

Your veins work hard to move blood from your feet back up to your heart. Gravity pulls the blood down, which puts pressure on the veins. This pressure can cause varicose veins. Treatment helps you feel better and improves the health of your legs and blood flow.

You may notice veins that look swollen, twisted, or like a spider's web. Your legs might feel heavy or achy, especially if you stand or sit for a long time.

You might have swelling in your ankles. The skin around the veins can feel itchy or like it is burning. Some people get leg cramps or feel like they need to move their legs, especially at night. These feelings can get worse if the veins are not treated.

Certain things can make you more likely to get varicose veins. Your chances increase as you get older, especially between ages 30 and 70. Pregnancy can also cause them, but they often go away after the baby is born.

Other things that increase your risk are having family members with varicose veins or being overweight. Having a job where you stand or sit for many hours can also be a cause.

To find out if you have varicose veins, you may see a special doctor called a vascular surgeon. This doctor focuses on blood vessels. The doctor will ask about your health, check your body, and look at your veins.

Your doctor might send you for a test called an ultrasound. This test does not hurt. It uses sound waves to create a picture of your veins and check how your blood is flowing inside them.

Your doctor will likely suggest some changes you can make at home first. Keeping a healthy weight can help. It is also helpful to raise your legs when you are resting.

Try not to sit or stand in one place for too long. Your doctor may also suggest you wear special tight socks, called compression stockings, to help with blood flow.

If these changes are not enough, your doctor might suggest a procedure. One option is sclerotherapy, which is often used for smaller spider veins. A doctor injects a special medicine into the vein to seal it shut.

For larger varicose veins, a procedure called vein stripping may be used. The doctor makes small cuts in the skin to remove the problem veins. You will be given medicine to make you sleep (general anesthesia) during this procedure.

Newer treatments use heat to close the vein. These are called radiofrequency or laser ablation. A doctor places a tiny tube with a special fiber into the vein. The fiber sends out energy that heats up and closes the bad part of the vein.
