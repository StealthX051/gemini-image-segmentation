The aorta is the largest blood vessel in your body. It carries blood from your heart down through your chest and into your belly. The part in your belly sends blood to your legs, stomach, and kidneys.

An abdominal aortic aneurysm, or AAA, is a weak, bulging spot in the wall of your aorta. If this bulge gets too big, it can burst. A burst aneurysm is very dangerous and can cause serious bleeding inside your body.

Many people are diagnosed with an AAA each year. A burst AAA can be life-threatening. Sometimes, blood clots can form in the aneurysm. These clots can travel and block blood flow to other parts of your body, like your legs.

Most of the time, an AAA does not cause any symptoms. It is often found by accident during a test for something else. If you do have symptoms, you might feel pain in your belly or groin. You might also notice a pulsing feeling in your belly, like a heartbeat.

An AAA happens when the wall of the aorta gets weak. Certain things can make you more likely to get an AAA. These are called risk factors.

Your risk is higher if you are over 60 years old or a man. Smoking, having high blood pressure, or having a family member with an AAA also increases your risk. Hardening of the arteries is another risk factor.

Medicare may cover a one-time screening test for an AAA. This is a free ultrasound test for some people when they first join Medicare. Men who have ever smoked and people with a family history of AAA may be able to get this screening.

If you have risk factors for an AAA, talk to your doctor. Your doctor may send you to see a vascular surgeon, who is a specialist in blood vessels.

To check for an AAA, your doctor may order an ultrasound of your belly. This test is painless and uses sound waves to see the aorta and measure the bulge. Your doctor might also order a special scan called a CTA to get a more detailed picture.

A vascular surgeon is an expert who decides if and when you need treatment. They will look at the size of the aneurysm and your health to find the best option for you. These surgeons are trained in different ways to fix an AAA.

If your aneurysm is small, your doctor may watch it closely. You might need to make changes like quitting smoking or controlling your blood pressure with medicine. You will have regular checkups to see if the aneurysm is growing.

If the aneurysm is large or growing, your doctor may recommend a repair. One type of repair involves placing a small tube inside the aorta to strengthen it. Another option is a surgery to replace the weak part of the aorta.
