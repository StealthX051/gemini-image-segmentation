Deep vein thrombosis, or DVT, is when a blood clot forms in a deep vein. A vein is a tube that carries blood. This often happens in a leg.

The clot can block blood from moving. If the clot breaks free, it can travel to your lungs. This is very dangerous and is called a pulmonary embolism. DVT is a serious problem. You need to see a doctor right away.

There are signs you may have a DVT. Your leg may swell up. It might hurt or feel sore, especially when you stand or walk.

The skin on your leg may feel warm or look red. The color of your skin might change to blue or red. Your leg may also feel tired or heavy.

If you have trouble breathing or have chest pain, get help right away. This could mean a clot has moved to your lungs.

You should talk to a doctor if you have any signs of a DVT. Tell the doctor if things in your life put you at risk for clots. Getting help early is very important to keep you safe.

Some things make you more likely to get a DVT. Not moving for a long time can cause a clot. This can happen on a long trip or if you are in bed after surgery.

Having surgery or getting hurt can also raise your risk. Being very overweight, being pregnant, or smoking can make clots more likely.

Some medicines, like birth control pills, can raise your risk. If people in your family have had blood clots, you might have a higher risk. Older people and people with other health problems are also at a higher risk.

A special doctor can find out if you have a DVT. The doctor will ask you questions and look at your leg.

You may have a blood test to check for a clot. The doctor will likely use a machine to look inside your leg. This test is called an ultrasound and it uses sound waves to make a picture.

Sometimes other tests are needed to see the veins better. Finding a clot early helps you get the right care.

Your doctor will decide the best treatment for you. Most people take medicine called blood thinners. This medicine helps stop the clot from getting bigger and stops new clots from forming.

You may need to wear special tight socks. These help the blood in your legs move better. They can also help with swelling and pain.

Keeping your leg raised up can also help with swelling. For very bad clots, a doctor may give you strong medicine to break up the clot.

In some cases, a small filter is put in a large vein. This filter can catch clots before they go to your lungs. Sometimes, a doctor may need to remove the clot.

It is important to follow your doctor's plan. This will help you get better and prevent future clots.
