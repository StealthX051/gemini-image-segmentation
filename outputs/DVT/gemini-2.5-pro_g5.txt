Deep vein thrombosis, or DVT, is a serious condition where a blood clot forms in a deep vein. This usually happens in the leg. The clot can block blood from flowing properly.

Sometimes, a piece of the clot can break off and travel to the lungs. This is a very dangerous problem called a pulmonary embolism. DVT is a medical emergency, so it is important to get help right away.

You might have a DVT if you notice signs in one of your legs. Look for swelling, pain, or soreness. The area might also feel warm or look red.

Your skin might change color, or your leg may feel heavy. If a clot travels to your lungs, you might suddenly feel short of breath or have chest pain. You should get medical help immediately if this happens.

You should see a doctor right away if you have any signs of a DVT. Tell your doctor about anything that might put you at risk for blood clots. Getting checked early can prevent serious problems.

Many things can raise your risk for a DVT. Not moving for a long time, like on a long trip or after surgery, is a common cause. An injury to your leg can also cause a clot.

Being overweight, pregnant, or smoking can increase your risk. Some medicines, like birth control pills, can also be a factor.

Your risk is also higher if you are over 60 years old or if blood clots run in your family. Certain health problems like cancer or heart disease can also lead to DVT.

A doctor who specializes in blood vessels, called a vascular surgeon, can find out if you have a DVT. The doctor will ask about your health and look at your leg for signs of a clot.

You may have a blood test to look for signs of a clot. The most common test is an ultrasound, which uses sound waves to create a picture of the blood flow in your veins. Sometimes, other picture-taking tests like a CT scan or MRI are used.

Your doctor will choose the best treatment for you. Most people take medicines called blood thinners. These drugs help stop the clot from getting bigger and prevent new clots from forming.

Your doctor might tell you to wear special tight stockings. These help with swelling and improve blood flow in your leg. Keeping your leg raised up can also help.

In serious cases, a doctor may use strong medicine to break up the clot quickly. Another option for some people is a small filter placed in a large vein. This filter can catch clots before they reach the lungs.

In rare cases, surgery may be needed to remove a very large blood clot. It is important to follow your doctor's plan and go to all your follow-up visits to stay healthy.
