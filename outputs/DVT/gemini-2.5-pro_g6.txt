Deep vein thrombosis, or DVT, is a serious condition where a blood clot forms in a deep vein. These veins are usually in your legs. This clot can block the blood flow in the vein, which can cause pain, swelling, and other problems.

If a piece of the clot breaks off and travels to your lungs, it can cause a dangerous blockage called a pulmonary embolism. This can be deadly. DVT is a medical emergency, so it is important to get it checked and treated right away to avoid serious problems.

You might notice swelling in one of your legs, often in the calf or thigh. The leg may feel painful or tender, especially when you stand or walk. The skin over the area might feel warm or look red or blue.

You might also see swollen veins or feel that your leg is heavy and tired. If you suddenly have trouble breathing, chest pain, a fast heartbeat, or cough up blood, get medical help right away. These can be signs of a clot in your lungs.

You should talk to a doctor if you have any signs of a DVT, like swelling, pain, or redness in one leg. Be sure to tell your doctor if you have things that make you more likely to get a DVT. Getting checked by a doctor quickly is the best way to find a DVT early.

Many things can raise your risk for DVT. Not moving for a long time, like on a long flight or resting in bed after surgery, is a major risk. Having surgery or an injury to a vein can also cause a clot.

Being overweight, being pregnant, or taking certain medicines like birth control pills can also increase your risk. Smoking damages your blood vessels and is another risk factor. Your risk is also higher if you are over 60 or if family members have had blood clots.

A special doctor will find out if you have a DVT. First, your doctor will ask about your symptoms and your health history. They will also check your leg for signs like swelling, pain, or redness.

A blood test may be done to look for signs of a clot. Your doctor will likely also use a test that lets them see inside your veins. The most common test is an ultrasound, which uses sound waves to create a picture of the blood flow.

The main treatment for DVT is medicine called anticoagulants, or blood thinners. These medicines stop the clot from getting bigger and prevent new clots from forming. You might get these as a shot at first, and then take them as pills.

Your doctor may also tell you to wear special tight socks called compression stockings. These socks help reduce swelling and pain by improving blood flow in your leg. Keeping your leg raised up can also help with swelling.

In very serious cases, a doctor might use strong medicines to break up the clot quickly. If you cannot take blood thinners, your doctor might place a small filter in a large vein. This filter catches blood clots before they can travel to your lungs.

Sometimes, a doctor may need to remove a very large or dangerous clot. Your doctor will choose the best treatment for you. It is important to follow your doctor's instructions and go to all your follow-up appointments.
